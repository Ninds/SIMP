# Mathematics and Physics: Intuition First

A topic-organised collection of gentle tutorials, derivations, and worked problems in mathematics and physics. Each chapter motivates the question first, develops the ideas slowly, and checks the result against geometry, dimensions, or a familiar special case.

## Purpose and scope

These documents are **not intended to be rigorous, comprehensive, or formal lecture notes**, nor are they an attempt to provide a rigorous treatise. Their primary purpose, more than anything else, is to **stimulate discussion**: to provide motivation, suggest ways of thinking about unfamiliar ideas, expose points of confusion, and give students and teachers something concrete to question together. Definitions, theorems, and proofs should ultimately be checked against the relevant lectures and rigorous textbooks.

Unlike formal mathematics or physics notes, these explanations are deliberately **subjective rather than objective**. The mathematics and physics themselves are not being presented as matters of opinion; the subjectivity lies in the route taken towards understanding them. Every mathematician or physicist may build a different mental model of the same area. One person may reason mainly by analogy, another visually or geometrically, another through examples, computation, physical intuition, or formal structure. No single explanatory route is expected to work equally well for everyone.

The analogies, diagrams, examples, ordering, and emphasis in this collection therefore reflect one particular mental model. They are conversation starters and invitations to think and ask questions, not substitutes for formal teaching.

The author created these notes solely to supplement the lectures attended by his son, a mathematics and physics undergraduate. They are shared in case that personal, intuition-first perspective also helps other students begin a topic and discuss it more productively.

## AI-assisted preparation

A large language model (LLM) was used to help prepare this collection. Its assistance included drafting and expanding some explanations, reorganising material, suggesting examples and diagrams, editing prose, creating cross-references, and producing or converting LaTeX and Markdown. The author directed the purpose and emphasis of the notes and selected the material included in the collection.

LLM assistance is not a guarantee of correctness. Mathematical and physical statements may still contain mistakes, omissions, or misleading analogies and should be checked against lectures, textbooks, and independent reasoning. The use of an LLM is disclosed here so that readers can assess the notes with appropriate context.

## Repository principles

- Intuition before formalism.
- Every symbol introduced before use.
- Derivations include intermediate steps.
- Diagrams stay in the PDF and editable-source editions; Markdown supplies a readable text alternative.
- Institution-neutral titles and metadata.
- No claim that a reconstructed edition is an unavailable original.
